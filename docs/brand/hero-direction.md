# Hero Direction

Version: 1.0

---

# Purpose

This document defines the strategic direction for the Hero section of the WIL.DEV homepage.

The Hero is the most important section of the website.

It establishes the first impression, introduces the brand and communicates the core value proposition.

Every visual and textual decision should reinforce one simple idea:

> Engineering Complex Solutions.

---

# Primary Goal

The Hero should immediately answer three questions.

Who is WIL.DEV?

↓

What does WIL.DEV do?

↓

Why should visitors care?

Users should understand the purpose of the platform within a few seconds.

---

# Emotional Goal

The Hero should create one dominant feeling.

> Confidence.

Not excitement.

Not curiosity.

Not surprise.

Confidence.

Visitors should feel they have arrived at the website of an experienced engineering professional.

---

# Communication Goals

The Hero should communicate:

- engineering maturity
- technical excellence
- thoughtful problem solving
- enterprise experience
- trust

Technology should never become the protagonist.

Engineering should.

---

# Content Hierarchy

The Hero follows a strict hierarchy.

Eyebrow

↓

Headline

↓

Supporting Statement

↓

Primary Call To Action

↓

Secondary Call To Action

↓

Supporting Visual

This hierarchy should remain consistent across all screen sizes.

---

# Headline

The headline should be short.

Clear.

Confident.

Examples of direction:

Engineering Complex Solutions.

Engineering Beyond Code.

Engineering Clarity from Complexity.

The final headline should communicate engineering rather than technology.

---

# Supporting Statement

The supporting copy expands the headline.

It should explain:

- what WIL.DEV is
- who it helps
- how it creates value

Avoid marketing language.

Prefer engineering language.

---

# Calls To Action

The Hero should contain two actions.

Primary

Explore Case Studies

or

View My Work

Secondary

Read Articles

or

About Me

CTAs should encourage exploration rather than conversion.

---

# Visual Direction

The visual should reinforce the message.

Not compete with it.

Possible directions:

- architectural diagrams
- system maps
- abstract geometry
- engineering grids
- blueprint-inspired graphics
- structured compositions

Avoid decorative illustrations.

---

# Layout

Desktop

---------------------------------------------------

Headline

Supporting Copy

Actions

↓

Visual Element

---------------------------------------------------

Mobile

Headline

↓

Supporting Copy

↓

Actions

↓

Visual

The message always comes before the visual.

---

# Background

The Hero background should remain subtle.

Prefer:

- deep neutral colors
- minimal gradients
- architectural patterns
- extremely subtle texture

Avoid distracting backgrounds.

---

# Motion

Motion should communicate refinement.

Possible interactions:

- soft fade-ins
- gentle parallax
- progressive reveal
- smooth transitions

Avoid dramatic animations.

---

# Scroll Experience

The Hero should naturally invite scrolling.

Avoid explicit visual cues such as bouncing arrows.

Curiosity should drive exploration.

---

# Hero Height

The Hero should occupy approximately one viewport height.

It should feel spacious.

Never crowded.

---

# Supporting Elements

Optional elements include:

- trust indicators
- featured technologies
- certifications
- years of experience

These should remain secondary to the main message.

---

# Typography

The headline should dominate.

Supporting copy should provide context.

Buttons should remain visually balanced.

Typography establishes hierarchy.

---

# Whitespace

Whitespace is part of the Hero.

Generous spacing communicates confidence.

Avoid compact layouts.

---

# Accessibility

The Hero must support:

- keyboard navigation
- readable typography
- sufficient contrast
- semantic structure

Accessibility begins on the first screen.

---

# Success Criteria

The Hero is successful if visitors immediately understand:

- this is an engineering platform
- this person solves complex challenges
- the content is worth exploring
- the interface inspires confidence

---

# Anti-Patterns

Avoid Heroes that feel like:

- startup landing pages
- SaaS marketing websites
- agency templates
- personal portfolios
- gaming interfaces

The Hero should feel timeless.

---

# Long-Term Vision

The Hero should remain relevant as the platform evolves.

New technologies may appear.

The engineering mindset should remain unchanged.

---

# Creative North Star

Every decision made within the Hero should answer one question:

Does this reinforce the idea of Engineering Complex Solutions?

If not, it should be redesigned.

---

# Final Principle

The Hero is not an introduction.

It is a statement.

Visitors should leave the first screen with one clear impression:

> This is someone I would trust to solve complex engineering challenges.
